## Accepted artifact-scale performance

The dense-call stress input is `3,597,499` bytes, has `5,374` defined functions, and contains roughly `251,000` rendered direct-call occurrences.

- The original Starshine plain-DAE implementation exceeded the bounded `1500`-second run without producing output.
- The final seven-run Starshine median is `1.893s`; the observed runs were `1.883s`, `1.893s`, `1.899s`, `1.903s`, `1.888s`, `1.885s`, and `1.897s`.
- Binaryen v131's seven-run median is `0.356s`.
- Starshine output is externally valid with all features and remains byte-identical to the accepted clean endpoint at SHA-256 `fa73c6aac3c93ac9c97bd261ce24403c4b7cfb8fbdb417f4ed74e1488caf11a9`.

Release review explicitly accepts this remaining performance gap. The known remaining opportunities are dominated by raw cleanup, eager original/boundary analysis, and final debris scanning. Prior experiments showed that tighter micro-gates, fused raw traversal, scratch pooling, narrower validation, and retaining complete post-transaction graphs were neutral or regressive; further material improvement would require a broader architectural rewrite with increased correctness and GC-lifetime risk. Because Starshine performs materially stronger cleanup on this artifact and no semantic, validity, lifecycle, or size blocker remains, that risk is not justified for v0.1.1.

Future fused-analysis or indexed raw-cleanup work may be pursued as non-blocking optimization, but there is no active DAE-owned release blocker.
